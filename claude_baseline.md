## Part 1: The Role I Want

### My Thesis on Selling the Agency-to-TAS Transition

The conventional wisdom on hybrid services + product companies says pick a lane. I think that's wrong for Will Be Live specifically — because the agency *is* the proof for the products. WillFlow's credibility comes from "we built this to run our own enterprise SEO operation." LiveLink's credibility comes from "we manage LinkedIn for B2B enterprises and built the tool we needed." Separate the two and both halves get weaker. The job isn't to resolve the tension between agency and SaaS — it's to sequence them.

What that means in practice is that the sales motion should be organized around buyer *intent to execute*, not company size:

- **Buyers who want it done for them** → Agency retainer, with WillFlow or LiveLink embedded in delivery
- **Buyers who want to execute internally and have the team for it** → WillFlow or LiveLink standalone
- **Buyers who started with one and need the other** → cross-sell motion (this is where the highest LTV sits)
- **Enterprise B2B running LinkedIn campaigns** → LiveLink as the wedge, agency as the expansion

The player-coach question is real but the standard answer ("mornings sell, afternoons lead") collapses on first contact with a real week. The honest version: I own deals where the buyer expects to talk to an owner-equivalent — top of the ACV range or anything sourced through Nima's network. Everything else I coach. Time split flexes with pipeline. The discipline is hiring my replacement-as-seller from month one, not protecting selling time on a calendar.

### What I Know About Will Be Live

Will Be Live's edge is three things competitors can't easily copy: Nima's media and relationship surface area, a delivery track record at enterprise scale, and a real product bet most agencies don't have. WebFX, Directive, and Power Digital compete on services depth but don't own product — which means they can't tell a multi-year compounding story to a buyer or to themselves. The TAS thesis only wins if the sales team can explain *why* buying technology from the agency that built it is an advantage rather than a red flag: implementation expertise comes with the software, the strategic context is already loaded, and there's one accountable party for both.

## Part 2: 90-Day Sales Plan

### Days 1-30: Diagnose & Recover Stuck Revenue

**Week 1: Get the Data Before the Vibes**

| Day | Action |
|-----|--------|
| Mon | CEO alignment: top three priorities, deal history, internal politics, what's actually broken vs. what feels broken |
| Tue | Read every won and lost deal from the last 6 months in CRM before sitting on any live call. Pattern recognition comes from the dataset, not from two shadowed calls |
| Wed | Product deep-dive: WillFlow with product team, LiveLink with CS. Specifically: where does each product break down in implementation? |
| Thu | Pipeline audit: every open deal categorized by *real* status, not CRM stage |
| Fri | 1:1 with each rep. One question matters: do I trust them with a $50K+ deal unsupervised? |

**Weeks 2-4: Recover the Deals That Should Have Closed**

Every pipeline I've seen has two or three deals stuck not because they're dead, but because nobody pushed. Finding those is where a new sales leader earns trust fast.

| Category | Criteria | Action |
|----------|----------|--------|
| Recoverable | Champion still engaged, blocker is identifiable, decision-maker reachable | I personally re-open within week 2 |
| Live but unqualified | In pipeline but discovery never confirmed budget, timeline, or authority | Re-qualify or remove |
| Pipeline theater | Stalled 60+ days, no champion response, kept "open" for forecast optics | Archive same day |

### Days 31-60: Fix the One Leak That Matters Most

Most 90-day plans run parallel workstreams across outbound, inbound, process, and hiring. That's how you accomplish nothing in 90 days. I'd pick the single biggest leak from the month-one diagnosis and fix that before touching anything else.

My prior, before seeing the data: the biggest leak is the handoff between *interest* and *qualified pipeline*. Inbound from Nima's content and network arrives hot and decays fast. If response is slow or the first call is generic, the lead is dead and the team doesn't know it.

**If the leak is inbound conversion (most likely):**

| Fix | Action | Owner |
|-----|--------|-------|
| Speed-to-lead | All inbound responded to in under 5 minutes during business hours, under 30 outside. CRM routing + Slack alerts | Closer + me for enterprise |
| Discovery quality | Standardized framework: budget, timeline, authority, and the *real* problem confirmed in first 20 minutes | Every rep, coached weekly |
| Ruthless disqualification | If the deal doesn't fit ICP or buyer can't commit to a next step, it doesn't go in pipeline | Me, in weekly reviews |

**If the leak is proposal-to-close:** rework the proposal motion — multi-thread earlier, executive alignment before proposal goes out, structured close plans for every deal over a threshold.

**If the leak is expansion:** build the cross-sell motion described in days 61-90 earlier than planned.

**Outbound Strategy (parallel but secondary in this phase)**

| Channel | Volume | Owner | Tool |
|---------|--------|-------|------|
| Signal-based outbound (agency) | Quality over volume, ~50 high-fit prospects/week | BDR | Apollo + Clay + AI signal monitoring |
| LinkedIn outreach (WillFlow) | 50 connections/week | Sales Ops hybrid | Sales Nav + Clay |
| Warm intro / network-sourced | 10/week | Me | Nima's network + CRM referral triggers |
| Inbound response | All leads < 5 min | Closer + me for enterprise | CRM routing + Slack alerts |

**Sales Process by Product**

Agency:
1. Discovery (30 min) → 2. Audit/strategy session (45 min) → 3. Proposal presentation → 4. Executive alignment → 5. Close
- Cycle: 60-90 days
- Key: multi-thread to CMO/VP early, never single-threaded past discovery

WillFlow:
1. Demo (20 min) → 2. Trial/pilot (14 days) → 3. ROI review → 4. Close
- Cycle: 14-30 days
- Key: usage in week one or the deal is dead

LiveLink:
1. Discovery + demo (30 min) → 2. Pilot campaign (30 days) → 3. Results review → 4. Expand
- Cycle: 21-45 days
- Key: measured lift against their native LinkedIn baseline, not against zero

**Rep Assessment by Day 45**

The real question isn't activity, it's whether a rep can run a deal I couldn't run myself. Activity is the floor; deal ownership is the ceiling.

| Signal | Action |
|--------|--------|
| Owns complex deals end-to-end, brings me in deliberately | Promote: bigger deals, mentorship role |
| Hits activity, loses on conversion | Coach: discovery depth, multi-threading, objection handling |
| Coachable but inconsistent execution | 30-day improvement plan with specific deal-level goals |
| Not coachable or pattern-of-one losing | Replace, no extended PIP — fast moves both ways are kinder than slow ones |

### Days 61-90: Build the Second Motion

By day 60 the primary leak is fixed or measurably improving. Days 61-90 add the *second* revenue motion deliberately — usually outbound, because inbound from Nima's content is unpredictable and the business can't compound on a single channel.

The outbound bet for Will Be Live can't be volume. WebFX sends more emails than we ever will. The bet is *specificity*: target accounts where a visible signal indicates real need.

| Signal | Play | Owner |
|---------|-----------|-------|
| B2B SaaS company hires Head of Content / SEO Director | WillFlow outreach within 48 hours — new leader has budget and is looking for wins | BDR (monitor) → Me (close) |
| B2B company running visible LinkedIn campaigns with weak engagement | LiveLink audit offer with specific examples from their ad library | Sales Ops (identify) → Closer |
| Funding announcement at agency-ICP company | Agency out
