# Scoring Rubric: Sales Director 010

This challenge-specific rubric supplements the scoring.md 

## Challenge-Specific Evaluation Points

### Will Be Live Specificity (25 points)

**What we're looking for:**
- Evidence of real research (knows Nima Mirpourian's content, studied the agency's positioning, identifies actual competitors)
- Specific references to WillFlow, LiveLink, and how the agency monetizes across services and product
- Understanding of the agency-to-TAS transition challenge — why selling both is structurally harder than selling either alone
- Named competitors (WebFX, Directive, Power Digital, or others), market dynamics, or realistic pricing context

**Red flags:**
- Could apply to any agency or SaaS company without changing a word
- No mention of Nima Mirpourian, the agency's media surface area, or how inbound actually arrives
- Generic "digital marketing agency" framing with interchangeable nouns
- No grasp of why TAS (Technology-Augmented Services) is structurally different from either pure agency or pure SaaS

### Strategic Depth (25 points)

**What we're looking for:**
- 90-day plan with real mechanics — what gets done, in what order, and why that order
- Distinct sales motions for high-value agency retainers vs. WillFlow vs. LiveLink, with cycle lengths and deal shapes that reflect reality
- Realistic ramp assumptions with specific numbers, not aspirational round figures
- Cross-sell motion between the three product lines, with triggers and ownership
- Rep assessment framework that goes beyond activity metrics — specifically, can this rep run a deal you couldn't?

**Red flags:**
- Identical sales process across agency, WillFlow, and LiveLink
- No pipeline targets, outbound volume figures, or speed-to-lead commitments
- "Build relationships" or "drive alignment" without specific tactics underneath
- Generic MEDDIC/BANT copy-paste with no adaptation to Will Be Live's actual buyer
- No serious thinking on how the player-coach time split actually flexes with pipeline reality

### AI Fluency (20 points)

**What we're looking for:**
- Real AI workflows with before/after metrics, not just "we use Claude"
- Specific tools beyond ChatGPT (Clay, Apollo, signal-monitoring agents, transcript analysis)
- AI-powered prospecting sequence detailed enough that a rep could execute it Monday morning
- Thoughtful position on where AI changes the GTM Director role specifically, not just sales generally
- Honest read on where AI fails — buying-committee politics, champion trust, negotiation timing

**Red flags:**
- "I use ChatGPT for emails" with no workflow underneath
- Anti-AI posture or skepticism at an AI-native agency (instant disqualifier)
- Generic AI tool list with no integration story
- A prospecting sequence whose only AI component is "use AI to personalize"
- Treating pre-call research as the AI edge — that's 2024 thinking

### Sales Judgment (20 points)

**What we're looking for:**
- Correct prioritization — recover stuck pipeline before chasing new logos
- Real grasp of enterprise sales cycles vs. SaaS sales cycles and why they require different motions
- Realistic quota ramp that reflects 60-90 day agency cycles
- Rep assessment that's honest about replacement, not just "coach everyone"
- Recognition of the structural tension between carrying personal quota and managing a team — and a credible answer for it

**Red flags:**
- Day 1 plan starts with "hire more reps"
- No urgency on existing pipeline; treats it as background noise
- Applies the same sales process to agency, WillFlow, and LiveLink
- Close timelines that don't pass the smell test (enterprise deals don't close in 30 days)
- No contingency for underperforming reps or no willingness to make a hard call

### Communication (10 points)

**What we're looking for:**
- Scannable structure with tables, clear headers, and tight sections
- Respects the page limit (8 pages total)
- Direct, specific language — owner/operator voice
- Numbers and metrics doing real work, not decorating the prose

**Red flags:**
- Wall-of-text essay format
- Exceeds the page limit
- Buzzword-heavy prose with no substance underneath
- No visual organization — tables, lists, or frameworks
- Reads like a consulting deck rather than a builder's plan

## Hints for What Beats Claude

Claude's baseline answer is structured and methodical:
- Clean 90-day framework with three logical phases
- Reasonable ICP definitions for each product
- Standard signal-based outbound and metrics dashboard
- Competent AI fluency section that goes one step beyond table stakes

- Show a specific outbound sequence for WillFlow or LiveLink with the trigger logic, the email copy, and the follow-up cadence (not just "we personalize with AI")
- Include actual talk tracks, objection-handling language, or proposal structures you'd use
- Demonstrate you've consumed Nima Mirpourian's content and understand where his thinking is going, not just where it's been
- Propose a cross-sell motion sharper than the one in the benchmark — with specific triggers, timing, and ownership splits
- Show how you've personally used AI to close more deals with real numbers attached
- Take on the hardest part directly: how you hit personal quota while ramping a team without letting one collapse the other
- Disagree with the benchmark on something specific and defend the disagreement — that's the strongest signal possible

**Claude's answer tends to be:**
- Structurally sound but still generic enough that minor edits would make it fit a different agency
- Missing the texture of someone who's actually built a sales org through a quarter where everything went wrong
- Correct frameworks without the earned intuition for which framework to apply when
- Strong on theory, weaker on the execution details that only come from having done it

The bar to beat is "operator who studied sales management." To win, show us you've actually carried a bag while running a team — and that you know the specific tradeoffs that requires when one of the products you're selling is software, the other is services, and the buyer doesn't always know which one they need.
